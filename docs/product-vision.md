# Product Vision — EU Fit Check

## 1. Vision & Mission

### Vision Statement

A Thai professional who's ever typed "jobs in Germany" into a search bar has an honest, immediate answer to "am I actually ready for this?" — instead of guessing, Googling generic advice, or filling out a form that goes nowhere for days.

### Mission Statement

EU Fit Check turns a few minutes of mobile-friendly questions into an instant, visual, self-report readiness profile — spanning whichever relocation pathway the person is actually exploring — while quietly building PunProfile the qualified, nurturable lead record it needs to follow up well.

### Founder's Why

Paul didn't move to Europe by getting a job offer first. He moved to Spain via a Master's degree — a pathway that most career-readiness tools, including PunProfile's own existing survey funnel, don't model at all. He made that move without any honest, structured way to check his own readiness against what the EU market actually wanted from him. He's now building the tool he wishes had existed.

That personal history matters for more than the "study-first" pathway question in the MVP. It's the reason this product insists on honesty over flattery: a self-report chart that just tells people what they want to hear would have been useless to Paul when he was making this decision himself, and it would be useless to the Thai professionals he's now trying to serve.

It also explains the dual purpose baked into this product from day one. Paul isn't just a founder who wants adoption metrics — he's the person who personally works every lead this tool generates into a coaching conversation. The tool has to be honest with the candidate and useful to Paul simultaneously, or it fails at the one job it actually has.

### Core Values

- **Self-report and say so.** Every score this app shows is explicitly labeled as self-reported and preliminary. The temptation to make the chart *feel* more authoritative than the data behind it supports must always be resisted — this is the single fastest way to burn the trust PunProfile's whole coaching business depends on.
- **Value before ask.** Never request contact information before the user has seen something real. The teaser reveal comes first, every time — friction is earned by demonstrated value, not assumed as a toll gate.
- **More data, not different data, as the relationship deepens.** Each stage of the journey (teaser → email-unlocked profile → deeper return-visit profile → paid ECRA) asks for more of the same trust, not a bait-and-switch into unrelated data collection.
- **No pathway is the default pathway.** Job-first, study-first, family, and "not sure" are all first-class inputs, not a job-first form with an afterthought checkbox. This came directly out of Paul's own relocation history.
- **Build nothing Paul can't maintain alone, intermittently.** Every technical and process decision assumes zero ongoing engineering attention for weeks at a time. If a feature requires regular manual upkeep, it doesn't belong in v1.

### Strategic Pillars

- **Honesty over conversion optimization.** If a design choice would inflate the chart's apparent authority to drive more email captures, it loses to honesty every time — this product exists downstream of a paid coaching business whose credibility it must protect, not spend.
- **The magic moment ships in week one of usage, not month three.** The teaser reveal is the whole point; if the MVP can't produce it fast and reliably, the MVP is wrong, not the deadline.
- **Recoverable, not just completable.** A session that stops halfway is not a failure state to eliminate — it's an expected, designed-for outcome that still produces a nurturable partial lead.
- **This app replaces the Form; it does not compete with ECRA.** Anything that starts to look like a full coach-validated 34-competency assessment belongs in the paid engagement, not in this free tool.

### Success Looks Like

Twelve months from now, nobody at PunProfile remembers the Google Form. Every new lead from the Thai Jobs in Europe Facebook Group arrives through EU Fit Check on their phone, sees a partial chart within a minute of tapping the link, and either bounces (a real, acceptable outcome) or hands over an email and keeps going. Paul opens a simple dashboard each morning and sees new leads sorted with enough context — pathway, self-report profile, contact details, consent status — that he can decide in seconds who to message first. A meaningful share of leads have also connected a LINE ID, feeding a LINE Official Account nurture sequence that didn't exist a year ago. The tool has directly produced paying coaching clients, and — just as importantly — it has produced enough real pathway data (job-first vs. study-first vs. other) that Paul can make an informed, deliberate call on whether the "education matchmaker" idea is worth building, instead of guessing.

## 2. User Research

### Primary Persona

Nueng, 29, a marketing manager at a mid-size Bangkok company with about five years of experience. She's competent and well-regarded at work, but restless — she's been quietly browsing "marketing jobs Netherlands" and "marketing jobs Germany" on her phone during lunch breaks for the better part of a year, without ever taking a real step. She's moderately tech-comfortable (uses LINE, Instagram, TikTok fluently; unlikely to fill out a desktop-oriented form on a laptop she rarely opens). Her current approach to the "am I ready" question is a mix of doom-scrolling LinkedIn job posts she doesn't apply to and asking a friend who moved to Sweden two years ago, whose advice is warm but wildly pathway-specific and not really applicable to Nueng's own situation. Emotionally, she's stuck between real ambition and real self-doubt — she suspects she might not be competitive, but has no structured way to find out that isn't either expensive (paying a coach cold) or embarrassing (asking in the Facebook group and getting generic replies). What would make her switch to something new: an answer that feels personal and specific to her within the first minute, with zero commitment required to get it.

### Secondary Personas

- **Paul, the coach.** He's the internal user of the lead-triage side of this product. He needs the auto-generated lead summary to be scannable in seconds — pathway, self-report score pattern, contact completeness, consent status — because his actual bottleneck isn't lead volume, it's his own time to review and respond to each one.
- **Already-warm DM leads.** People who've already messaged Paul directly in the Facebook Group before ever seeing EU Fit Check. For them the tool isn't a discovery moment — it's homework Paul sends them before a call, so the conversation starts from a shared, specific picture instead of Paul re-asking everything live.

### Jobs To Be Done

- **Functional:** "Help me find out, in minutes and on my phone, whether I'm actually competitive for the EU market given the specific way I'm planning to get there."
- **Functional:** "Help me understand what would move the needle, not just get a score I can't act on."
- **Emotional:** "Let me explore this ambition privately, without committing money or admitting to a coach (or myself) that I might not be ready yet."
- **Emotional:** "Make me feel like this assessment actually sees my specific situation — not a generic template that assumes I'm applying for jobs the normal way."
- **Social:** "If this is good, let me be the person in my friend group who found the tool that actually gave a straight answer, worth sharing in the group."

### Pain Points

1. **Severe, frequent — no honest self-assessment exists today.** Nueng's current options are a slow Google Form or anecdotal peer advice; neither gives her a structured, EU-market-specific answer. This is the core problem the product exists to solve.
2. **Moderate, frequent — existing tools assume job-first only.** Anyone exploring a study-first, family, or other pathway gets advice calibrated to a route they're not taking. Real but currently invisible, because nobody has asked the pathway question at all.
3. **Moderate, occasional — the current Form is long with no payoff until days later.** Not everyone abandons a 25-question form, but completion without instant feedback means the tool captures data without building any trust or momentum with the candidate.
4. **Minor, occasional — desktop-oriented forms don't fit how this audience actually browses.** A meaningful share of Facebook Group traffic is mobile-only in practice; a form that's merely "responsive" rather than genuinely mobile-first will quietly lose people who'd otherwise convert.

### Current Alternatives & Competitive Landscape

- **The existing PunProfile funnel (6-question quiz + 25-question Google Form).** Does well: it works, it's live, and it feeds a working lead qualification framework (Propensity, ICP, Country Fit). Falls short: no instant feedback, assumes job-first pathway, not built mobile-first, and there's no completion-rate data proving the quiz stage even reduces drop-off the way it was designed to.
- **Generic online career/personality quizzes (16Personalities-style).** Do well: instant, low-friction, shareable. Fall short: completely generic, no EU-market or Thai-specific relevance, no connection to an actual coaching framework behind the score.
- **LinkedIn's "profile strength" meter.** Does well: already embedded in a tool this audience already uses. Falls short: measures profile completeness, not job-market readiness or relocation-pathway fit — answers a different question entirely.
- **Peer advice / "do nothing."** Does well: free, trusted, emotionally comfortable. Falls short: wildly inconsistent, biased toward whatever pathway that one friend happened to take, and doesn't scale as a business input for PunProfile at all.

### Key Assumptions to Validate

- **We assume a self-report spider chart is enough of a "wow" to justify handing over an email.** We're assuming visual feedback beats form fatigue. To validate: track the teaser-to-email conversion rate in the first few weeks; if it's not meaningfully better than the current Form's completion rate, the teaser design needs rework before blaming the audience.
- **We assume the pathway-selector question (job-first/study-first/family/not sure) is understood without confusion.** To validate: watch for high "not sure" selection rates or support questions about what the options mean — a sign the framing needs simplifying.
- **We assume partial/abandoned sessions are actually recoverable leads PunProfile can nurture, not just noise.** To validate: track whether magic-link return rates and eventual completion rates from partial leads justify the extra build complexity of incremental save.
- **We assume replacing the Form immediately at launch (no parallel run) won't cause a lead-volume dip PunProfile can't absorb.** This is a real risk given no A/B data exists yet. To validate: watch weekly lead volume closely for the first month post-launch and be ready to revisit if it drops meaningfully.
- **We assume this audience is comfortable giving LINE ID and phone number at the email-unlock stage.** To validate: track field-level completion/skip rates once the app is live — if LINE ID or phone lag badly behind email, the ask may need to move later in the journey.
- **We assume Convex + Next.js + Vercel is genuinely maintainable by a non-technical founder using only an AI coding agent, with no engineering support, for weeks at a stretch.** This is the constraint the whole stack was chosen around; if it doesn't hold in practice, the roadmap should budget for a maintenance-simplification pass rather than silently absorbing bugs.

### User Journey Map

**Awareness:** Nueng sees the Free Consultation Hook at the end of a Thai Jobs in Europe Facebook post, or the pinned group post — the same discovery moment as today, just pointing at a different link.

**Consideration:** She taps the link on her phone, mildly skeptical (she's seen quiz links before that go nowhere useful). The page needs to signal in the first few seconds that this is specific and quick, not another generic form.

**First use:** She answers the first handful of questions, including the pathway question — a moment of relief if she's not on the job-first track most tools assume.

**Magic moment:** Within roughly a minute, part of her spider chart renders — real, visual, specific-feeling — before she's asked for anything. This is the moment that decides whether she continues.

**Habit formation (adapted for a low-frequency tool):** Rather than daily habitual use, "habit" here means she returns via her saved magic link to complete more of the profile, and eventually books the discovery call the tool has been quietly building toward.

**Advocacy:** If the experience felt honest and specific rather than generic, she shares the link in the Facebook group herself — the same organic loop that already drives PunProfile's existing content.

## 3. Product Strategy

### Product Principles

- **The teaser always comes before the ask.** No exceptions, no "just this one field first" creep — this is the single most load-bearing UX rule in the product.
- **Every score on screen is labeled self-reported.** The UI must never let a number look more authoritative than a self-report actually is.
- **Pathway shapes commentary, not just data collection.** Asking "job-first or study-first" only pays off if the resulting self-report narrative actually reads differently for each answer — otherwise it's a form field nobody benefits from.
- **A dropped-off session is a valid, designed-for outcome.** The product must save state incrementally and create a partial lead the moment minimum contact info exists — never treat "didn't finish" as an error state to eliminate.
- **Ship what one intermittently-available founder can run unattended.** Every feature is evaluated against: "if Paul disappears for three weeks, does this still work?"

### Market Differentiation

The existing alternatives fail this audience in one of two ways: they're either generic (personality quizzes, LinkedIn's meter) and ignore the EU-market and Thai-relocation specifics entirely, or they're specific but slow and one-directional (PunProfile's own current Form). EU Fit Check is the first tool in this space that's both instant and grounded — it inherits real scoring logic from PunProfile's coach-validated ECRA framework without pretending to replicate it, and it's honest about that boundary in the product itself rather than blurring it for a better-looking chart. That honesty is also the defensibility: a competitor could copy the spider-chart UI trivially, but they can't copy the years of real Thai-candidate data and a working coaching business standing behind the labels on that chart.

### Magic Moment Design

For the teaser reveal to happen reliably within about a minute, the first question set must be short (target: well under 10 questions before any reveal), require no typing beyond selecting options, and produce a chart segment from data that's genuinely available at that point — not a placeholder or a fake preview. The shortest path from landing to magic moment is: land → pathway question → 3–5 fast readiness questions → teaser chart segment renders immediately, client-side, with no waiting on a server round-trip that could stall the moment. If early build-out shows the teaser can't render this fast with real data, the MVP's first-question-set length is what shrinks — not the promise of an instant reveal.

### MVP Definition

**In scope:**

- **Progressive, mobile-first questionnaire with pathway selector.** Job-first / study-first / family / not sure, asked early, feeding into how later self-report commentary is worded. Essential because it's the single biggest gap in every existing alternative, including PunProfile's own Form. Done when a user can complete the full questionnaire on a phone with no layout or interaction issues, and the pathway answer visibly changes at least the narrative text in the final report.
- **Staged reveal: teaser before ask.** A partial chart renders from the first question set, before any email/contact request appears. Essential because it *is* the magic moment. Done when the teaser reliably renders within about a minute of starting, using real self-reported data, not a mock.
- **Self-report spider chart, clearly labeled.** Visual, multi-dimension chart summarizing self-reported readiness, with explicit "self-reported, preliminary" labeling throughout. Essential to the core value proposition and to protecting PunProfile's trust. Done when the chart renders correctly on mobile and desktop and the labeling is impossible to miss or misread as a validated score.
- **Progressive contact capture with PDPA-compliant consent.** Email requested to unlock the full chart; phone and LINE ID requested afterward as optional-but-encouraged fields, each with clear consent language. Essential because this is literally the lead-generation half of the product's dual purpose. Done when a lead record exists in the database the moment an email is captured, consent is logged with a timestamp, and phone/LINE ID can be added without re-starting the flow.
- **Partial-completion save/resume via magic link.** Anyone who stops mid-flow after giving an email can return later via a link sent to that email, picking up where they left off. Essential to the "recoverable, not just completable" principle. Done when a returning user via magic link sees their prior answers pre-filled and can continue or revise them.
- **Coach-facing lead dashboard.** A single authenticated view (Convex Auth, Paul as the only user) listing leads with pathway, self-report summary, contact completeness, and consent status, sorted by recency. Essential because a lead the coach can't act on efficiently defeats the CRM half of the product. Done when Paul can open the dashboard and identify, within seconds per lead, who to contact first.

**"Done" for the MVP as a whole:** the Google Form can be fully retired and 100% of new Thai Jobs in Europe leads can be routed through EU Fit Check without losing any capability PunProfile currently depends on for lead qualification.

### Explicitly Out of Scope

- **Full ECRA replication (all 34 competencies, coach-validated).** Tempting because it's the "complete" version of the same idea — but ECRA explicitly requires mock interviews, CV review, and coach judgment that a self-service form structurally cannot produce. Deferred permanently as a separate, paid, in-engagement deliverable, not just a later MVP phase.
- **Cold-traffic vs. warm-lead entry variants.** Tempting because the current Form already serves two different audiences differently in practice. Deferred until real usage data shows the single unified entry point is actually losing one segment — revisit after the first 90 days of data.
- **Education-matchmaker functionality (matching candidates to EU degree programs).** Tempting because it directly follows from Paul's own relocation story and the newly-flagged pathway gap. Deferred entirely — not scoped, not priced, not committed to. Revisit only after 6 months of real pathway-selector data shows enough study-first demand to justify scoping it as its own product decision.
- **LINE Official Account nurture automation.** Tempting because LINE ID capture is already in the MVP and nurture is an obvious next step. Deferred to the first post-launch phase — the MVP captures the data; wiring up automated nurture sequences is separate follow-on work once there's a real LINE ID pool to nurture.
- **Multi-country comparison / benchmarking against the anonymized lead pool.** Tempting because it would make the report feel richer. Deferred — needs a meaningful lead volume to be statistically honest, which doesn't exist at MVP launch.
- **Blockdit / Pantip content channels.** Tempting as GTM expansion. Deferred as "channels to test later," not part of this build at all — this is a marketing decision, not a product one, and shouldn't consume MVP engineering time.

### Feature Priority (MoSCoW)

- **Must Have:** Mobile-first progressive questionnaire; pathway selector; staged teaser-before-ask reveal; self-reported spider chart with explicit labeling; progressive contact capture with PDPA consent; partial-lead creation on minimum contact info; magic-link save/resume; coach-facing lead dashboard.
- **Should Have:** Automated new-lead email notification to Paul (via Resend); basic analytics funnel (PostHog) showing drop-off by stage; a simple "needs your screening" style flag on leads with incomplete or ambiguous data, mirroring the existing daily-jobs pipeline's pattern.
- **Could Have:** LINE Official Account nurture automation; richer self-report narrative variation by pathway beyond basic text substitution; lightweight export of lead data to a spreadsheet for ad hoc analysis.
- **Won't Have (this time):** Full ECRA replication; education-matchmaker features; multi-country benchmarking; cold/warm entry-point variants; any payment integration.

### Core User Flows

**Flow 1 — Candidate self-assessment (primary flow).**
Trigger: taps link from Facebook post or pinned post. Steps: lands on mobile-first entry screen → answers pathway question → answers first short question set → sees teaser chart segment → prompted for email to unlock full chart (consent captured here) → sees full self-report chart and narrative → optionally continues to deeper questions, or exits with a magic link saved to email → optionally provides phone/LINE ID → sees a clear next-step CTA to book a discovery call. Outcome: a self-report profile shown to the candidate, and a lead record created in the database the moment email is captured. Success criteria: teaser renders within about a minute; at least a defined target share of teaser-viewers convert to email capture (baseline to be set from first weeks of real data, not assumed up front).

**Flow 2 — Return via magic link.**
Trigger: candidate clicks the magic link from their earlier email. Steps: lands directly back into their in-progress or completed profile, pre-filled → continues answering deeper questions or reviews their existing result → optionally adds phone/LINE ID if not already given. Outcome: an enriched lead record with more complete data. Success criteria: prior answers are correctly restored with no data loss; no re-authentication friction beyond the link itself.

**Flow 3 — Coach lead triage.**
Trigger: Paul logs into the dashboard (Convex Auth). Steps: sees a list of leads sorted by recency, each showing pathway, self-report summary, contact completeness, and consent status → opens a lead for full detail → decides who to contact and how. Outcome: Paul's daily/weekly outreach list is generated without manual spreadsheet review. Success criteria: Paul can identify who to contact first without needing to open more than the summary view for most leads.

### Success Metrics

- **Primary metric:** number of discovery calls booked directly attributable to EU Fit Check. Good: 15 in the first 90 days. Great: 30+, with at least one paying client traceable to the tool.
- **Secondary metrics:** teaser-to-email conversion rate (good: comparable to or better than current Form completion rate; great: meaningfully higher, validating the "value before ask" bet); total assessment starts (good: 150 in 90 days; great: 300+, per the founder's stated ambitious target); email-to-phone/LINE-ID completion rate (signals whether the progressive ask is well-calibrated).
- **Leading indicators:** stage-by-stage drop-off in PostHog funnels (watch especially the pathway question and the email-unlock step); magic-link return rate for partial completions; ratio of "not sure" pathway answers (a high ratio may mean the pathway framing needs simplifying before it can meaningfully shape commentary).

### Risks

- **Risk: lead volume dips after retiring the Form immediately, with no parallel-run safety net.** Likelihood: moderate. Impact: high — this is PunProfile's only qualified lead channel. Mitigation: watch weekly volume closely in the first month; have a fast-rollback plan (temporarily reinstating the Form link) ready even though the intent is a clean cutover.
- **Risk: self-report scores get inflated by users gaming the chart to see a better result.** Likelihood: moderate-high (inherent to any self-report instrument). Impact: moderate — degrades lead-quality signal for Paul, though it doesn't undermine the paid ECRA process which stays separately coach-validated. Mitigation: explicit self-report labeling throughout; treat the app's self-report data as a triage signal for Paul, never as a substitute for his own judgment on a call.
- **Risk: the pathway question confuses users or gets ignored (defaults to "not sure" en masse).** Likelihood: moderate. Impact: moderate — if this happens, the pathway-aware commentary this MVP is built around doesn't actually differentiate the product. Mitigation: watch pathway-answer distribution closely post-launch; be ready to revise the question wording quickly.
- **Risk: a solo, non-technical, intermittently-available founder can't keep a live production app healthy.** Likelihood: moderate. Impact: high — an app that silently breaks while Paul is away is worse than the Form it replaced. Mitigation: Sentry error tracking from day one, and a strong bias in the roadmap toward managed services (Convex, Vercel) that don't require manual server maintenance.
- **Risk: PDPA consent handling is done informally and creates real legal exposure.** Likelihood: low-moderate (no consent framework exists in the current Form either, so this is a pre-existing gap being inherited, not created). Impact: high if it goes wrong. Mitigation: explicit, timestamped consent capture built into the MVP itself (already in scope above), not treated as a later nice-to-have.
- **Risk: the "education matchmaker" idea creates scope pressure to add it into this MVP.** Likelihood: moderate, given it's a compelling personal story for the founder. Impact: moderate — would blow the 4–8 week MVP budget. Mitigation: it's explicitly out of scope above with its own revisit trigger; treat any pressure to add it now as a signal to re-read that section, not a reason to build it.
- **Risk: mobile-first requirement gets under-tested and the actual experience degrades on real devices.** Likelihood: moderate. Impact: high, since this audience is disproportionately mobile-only. Mitigation: test on real phone form factors throughout the build, not just responsive breakpoints in a desktop browser.

## 4. Brand Strategy

### Positioning Statement

For Thai professionals who've decided Europe is the goal but don't know if they're actually competitive, EU Fit Check is the instant self-assessment tool that gives an honest, coach-grounded first read on EU job-market readiness — across whichever relocation pathway they're actually pursuing. Unlike generic online quizzes or a slow qualification form, EU Fit Check shows real visual feedback within a minute, grounded in PunProfile's actual coaching framework, while staying explicit that it's a preliminary self-report — not a substitute for the full validated assessment a coaching client receives.

### Brand Personality

EU Fit Check talks like the same warm, honest advisor PunProfile already is in its Facebook content — never a slick growth-hacked quiz app, never a cold corporate assessment tool. If this brand were a person, they'd be the friend who moved to Europe a couple of years ago and, when you ask "do you think I could actually do this?", doesn't just say "of course!" to be nice — they tell you what's genuinely working in your favor and what isn't, in plain language, because they respect you enough not to blow smoke. They'd never use corporate qualification language ("lead," "triage," "asset") anywhere the user can see it. They'd never inflate a score to make someone feel good in the moment at the cost of being straight with them later.

### Voice & Tone Guide

| Context | DO | DON'T |
|---|---|---|
| Onboarding | "A few quick questions, and you'll see part of your profile right away." | "Complete this comprehensive 25-point assessment to proceed." |
| Error states | "That didn't save — mind trying that one again?" | "Error: submission failed. Please retry." |
| Empty/loading states | "Building your first result now..." | "Loading... please wait." |
| Success messages | "Here's your first look — Mobility Readiness is shaping up well. Keep going to see the rest." | "Assessment segment 1 of 4 complete." |
| Marketing copy | "See where you actually stand for Europe — takes a few minutes, no commitment." | "Unlock your ultimate European career potential today!" |

All customer-facing copy is Thai-first, in natural conversational register — the same standard `03_Content_System.md` already holds PunProfile's Facebook content to, not literal-translated corporate English.

### Messaging Framework

- **Tagline:** "See where you actually stand for Europe."
- **Homepage headline:** "An honest first read on your EU job-market readiness — in a few minutes, on your phone."
- **Value propositions:**
  1. Instant, visual feedback instead of a form that goes nowhere for days.
  2. Grounded in a real coaching framework, not a generic personality quiz.
  3. Built for however you're actually getting to Europe — job offer, study, family, or still figuring it out.
- **Feature description example:** "Answer a few quick questions and see part of your profile immediately — before you give us anything."
- **Objection handler ("Is this just another quiz that means nothing?"):** "This isn't a personality quiz — it's built from the same framework PunProfile uses with real coaching clients, just the self-report starting point. We tell you clearly what's preliminary and what would need a real conversation to confirm."

### Elevator Pitches

- **5-second:** "An honest, instant readiness check for Thai professionals aiming for Europe."
- **30-second:** "Most people trying to move to Europe for work have no real way to check if they're competitive — they either fill out a slow form that goes nowhere, or take a generic quiz that ignores their actual situation. EU Fit Check gives an instant, visual self-assessment grounded in a real coaching framework, built for however someone's actually getting there — job offer, study, or otherwise — in just a few minutes on their phone."
- **2-minute:** "Paul moved to Europe himself — not through a job offer, but through a Master's degree — and had no honest way at the time to check whether he was actually ready for what he was attempting. Since then he's built PunProfile Career Coaching around a real, structured readiness framework, but the only way for a new lead to touch that framework has been a slow Google Form that gives nothing back until someone manually reads it days later. EU Fit Check replaces that form entirely: a Thai professional taps a link from the Facebook group, answers a short set of mobile-friendly questions — including which pathway they're actually exploring — and sees part of their readiness profile rendered visually within about a minute, before being asked for anything. If they want the full picture, they hand over an email and keep going; if they don't, no harm done. Every step of the way the app is explicit that this is a self-reported, preliminary read — not a replacement for the validated assessment a real coaching client gets. And on the other side, every completed or even partially-completed session becomes a lead Paul can actually act on, sorted and summarized, instead of a spreadsheet row he has to interpret cold. It's a small, focused tool, but it's the honest version of something Paul needed and didn't have."

### Competitive Differentiation Narrative

Every existing option in this space picks one side of a trade-off EU Fit Check refuses to accept. Generic quiz apps are instant and shareable but say nothing real about EU job-market readiness or relocation-pathway fit. PunProfile's own current Form says something real, backed by an actual lead-qualification framework, but takes 25 questions and days of silence to say it. EU Fit Check is built to be both: instant enough to hook a skeptical scroller in a Facebook feed, and honest enough to be genuinely grounded in the same framework a paying coaching client's assessment draws from — with the boundary between "quick self-report" and "full validated assessment" stated plainly rather than blurred for a better-looking chart. That honesty isn't just an ethical stance, it's the moat: a competitor can clone a spider-chart UI in a weekend, but they can't clone years of real Thai-candidate data and a working coaching business willing to stand behind what the chart says.
